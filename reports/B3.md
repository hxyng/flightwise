## Loop behavior
- Up to 6 `llm.chat` calls (to_thread, think=False). The 6th call goes without tools, so the model has to answer. If it gives no text, we send a stock "step limit" message.
- Events go in this order: `thinking` (if any), then `tool_call`, then `tool_result` with `{name, result, ok}`, then `message`, then `done` with `{history}`. If the LLM call fails we emit `error` and then `done`, without crashing.
- The UI gets the full result. The model gets compact JSON with nulls, `sql`, `source`, `id` and timestamps removed, lists capped at 5 (rows at 12) plus a `*_total` count, and floats rounded.
- An unknown tool, bad arguments or a backend exception turns into an `error: ...` string as the tool result. A failed risk lookup becomes `{"error": "delay history unavailable"}`.
- Text fallback: if `tool_calls` is empty, we parse (1) JSON `{"name","arguments"|"parameters"}` objects, including `{"function":{...}}` wrapping and fenced blocks, (2) Gemma native `call:name{k:<|"|>v<|"|>}`, and (3) Python-style `name(k="v")`. Only known tool names count.
- History: the system message is stripped and re-added each turn, the last 12 messages are kept, and the trimmed history always starts on a `user` message.
- Dates: the system prompt includes today plus the next 7 days with weekday names. `call_tool` also runs `resolve_date` on every `date` arg (today, tomorrow, Friday, fri, next monday, 11/3, ISO).
- **Timezone fix (not in the brief):** providers return UTC (`...Z`), but the BTS departure hour is local time. Before calling delay_risk, `_hour_of` converts to the origin's local hour using a ~70-airport IATA→tz map. For an unknown airport with a UTC time it sends `hour=None` rather than guess. The model also sees `*_local` time strings, because in the first run it quoted 22:43 UTC as the departure time.

## Prompt key behaviors
- FlightWise persona, with today's date injected.
- Every fact comes from a tool.
- On "track", call track_flight. If the user asked to be kept updated, also call watch_flight; otherwise offer it in one line.
- On "find a flight", call search_flights, and call delay_risk for top offers that have no risk. Recommend the most reliable option rather than the cheapest, and never call an "unknown" risk reliable.
- History questions go to query_delay_history; "what am I watching" goes to list_watched.
- Quote times from the `*_local` fields, and say history is unavailable when risk errored.
- Answer in short markdown, under about 100 words.

## Real end-to-end runs (gemma4:12b already loaded, B1 providers on mock, B2 nl2sql present)
1. "What's the status of AA100 and will it be late?" (85 s total)
   - `tool_call track_flight {flight_number: AA100, date: 2026-10-26}` at 20.5 s
   - `tool_result` (ok) at 24.4 s
   - `message` at 85.4 s: boarding, on time, dep 18:46 local CLT, gate B35, **delay risk high, 48.4% delayed ≥15 min**, and it offered to watch the flight
   - `done` (history 4 messages)
   An earlier run, before nl2sql existed: track_flight at 15.7 s, then the message at 100 s. Risk was an import error and the model handled it.
2. "Find me a flight OKC to DEN Friday" (149 s total)
   - `tool_call search_flights {OKC, DEN, 2026-10-30}` at 45 s (date resolved correctly)
   - `tool_result` (ok)
   - `message` at 149 s: a 4-row table with risk, recommending NK1704 nonstop at $81 over F9 at $65 with 1 stop and 26% risk
   - `done`
   The model did **not** call delay_risk for the offers whose risk was "unknown". It then recommended a Spirit flight whose risk was unknown. I tightened the prompt afterwards, but have not re-run it.

**Latency:** most of the time is final-answer generation, 60–104 s for 100–200 words. The tool-choice call takes 15–45 s. The orchestrator should consider a `num_predict` cap or streaming the final answer in `llm.py`, which I don't own.

## Not verified
- The text-fallback path against the real model. It never triggered, so it is covered only by unit tests on synthetic strings.
- Watch chaining ("track AA100 and keep me updated" → watch_flight) and query_delay_history with the real model. I didn't run them, partly because I wasn't sure watcher.py was finished.
- Whether the prompt tweaks made after the runs (under ~100 words, "unknown" risk not reliable) change the model's behavior.
- Risk lookups whose hour/carrier don't match BTS coverage, such as non-US airports. Those fall back to label "unknown" or an error.
- Concurrent use by other builders' processes, which could explain the variable step times.
